## 1. The Sikkim tourism ecosystem now

### 1.1 Arrival volumes and trend

Figures released by the Tourism & Civil Aviation Department in early January 2026, as reported
by Travel And Tour World (7 Jan 2026) and EastMojo (7 Jan 2026):

| Year | Total | Domestic | Foreign |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 16,25,241 | 15,40,421 | 84,820 |
| 2025 | 17,12,360 | 16,35,650 | 61,710 |

- Total arrivals grew **+5.4%** year on year (17,12,360 / 16,25,241).
- Domestic arrivals grew **+6.2%** (+95,229 visitors).
- **Foreign arrivals fell 27.2%**, from 84,820 to 61,710 — a loss of 23,110 visitors, attributed
  by the department chiefly to a fall in arrivals from Bangladesh.
- Source: https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/sikkim-tourism-shows-resilience-in-2025-with-over-seventeen-lakh-visitors-domestic-travel-growth-outpaces-foreign-arrivals/ (retrieved 2026-08-24);
  https://eastmojo.com/sikkim/2026/01/07/sikkim-records-17-12-lakh-tourists-in-2025-foreign-tourists-dip/ (headline figures via search index; direct fetch returned HTTP 403 on 2026-08-24);
  https://northeasttoday.in/northeast/sikkim-records-over-17-lakh-tourist-arrivals-in-2025/ (403 on direct fetch 2026-08-24).

**Caution — an inconsistency in the reporting.** The same article states foreign arrivals fell
"by approximately 10,000 to 12,000" and then gives 84,820 → 61,710, a fall of 23,110. The two
statements cannot both be right. Use the tabulated year totals, not the prose delta, and treat
the foreign-arrival decline as approximately 23,000 with that caveat noted.

**Scale context.** Sikkim's population at the 2011 Census was 6,10,577
(https://censusindia.gov.in — Sikkim state totals). Against 17.1 lakh annual arrivals in 2025,
that is roughly **2.8 visitors per resident per year**. No more recent official population count
exists (the 2021 Census was not conducted), so this ratio uses a 2011 denominator and is a floor
on the per-resident load, not a precise figure.

**2026 projection.** The Additional Chief Secretary, Tourism, was reported as expecting arrivals
"close to 20 lakh in 2026" on improved NH-10 conditions. This is a departmental expectation, not
a measured figure — treat it as such.

### 1.2 Seasonality

Month-level figures reported for 2025 vs 2024:

| Month | 2025 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| October | 77,522 | 18,134 |
| November | 1,77,577 | 2,03,062 |
| December | 2,39,701 | 2,25,332 |

- **December is the annual peak** (2,39,701 in 2025 — 14.0% of the year's total in one month).
- The October 2024 figure of 18,134 is an outlier collapse: roughly **one-quarter** of the
  October 2025 figure. October 2024 falls in the window of NH-10 / Teesta corridor road damage
  following the 2023 GLOF and the 2024 monsoon damage (see §5). Seasonality in Sikkim is
  therefore **compounded by access risk**: a bad road month does not merely shift demand, it
  deletes it.
- Source: same Travel And Tour World article, retrieved 2026-08-24.
- **No published month-by-month series for the full 12 months of 2025 was found** in any
  accessible source; only Oct/Nov/Dec were reported. Treat any full-year seasonality curve as
  unsourced unless the department's own statistics handbook is obtained.

### 1.3 District concentration and the state's dispersal ambition

The registers measured in this repo show the concentration directly: **74.6% of registered hotels
and 83.3% of registered travel agents sit in Gangtok district**, against 26.4% of the state's
catalogued heritage sites. Mangan district carries 24.5% of the heritage and 2.2% of the hotels.

The state's own stated direction is dispersal. Reported infrastructure and product pushes include
North Sikkim route restoration (Naga–Lachung reopened; Taram Chu bridge on the Lachen route
targeted for completion by 31 January 2026, restoring Gurudongmar Lake access) — see §5.
**No published state document quantifying a district-wise dispersal target (e.g. "X% of arrivals
outside Gangtok by year Y") was found.** That is a genuine gap: the ambition is stated in speeches
and infrastructure spending, not in a measurable published target.

Government of Sikkim (PDF via National Single Window System),
https://www.nsws.gov.in/s3fs/2022-12/Sikkim_Tourism_Policy_2018.pdf (retrieved 2026-08-24).

Supply-side counts as of the department's **Carrying Capacity Survey 2017** (Policy §3.3):

| Category | Count (2017) |
|---|---|
| Hotels | 1,021 (16,945 rooms; 34,133 beds) |
| Hotels at project/under-construction stage | 47 |
| Travel agents | 1,119 |
| Restaurants | 69 |
| Tourist guides | 66 |
| Homestays | 371 |
| Luxury taxis (as on 31-03-2015, RTA/MV Dept) | 646 |
| Local (non-luxury) taxis (as on 31-03-2015) | 6,445 |

Economy figures from the same section:
- Tourism GSDP 2016-17: **Rs 1,44,735 lakh**, **7.68% of GSDP** (source cited in policy: DESME
  State Income Unit).
- Direct employment "roughly estimated to be around **12,000 to 15,000 jobs**" — the policy itself
  labels this an estimate.
- **61% of direct workers are employed from outside the State** (source cited: Sikkim Human
  Development Report 2014).
- The policy's stated cause: *"a majority of hotels in the State are leased out to parties outside
  Sikkim who prefer to source their workers from outside the State."*
- Tourism sector growth rate over the preceding decade: **10-12%**.

More recent GSDP claim: Chief Minister P.S. Golay stated in April 2025 that tourism accounts for
about **10% of Sikkim's GSDP**, reported by Business Standard, 13 April 2025,
https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/tourism-accounts-for-10-of-sikkim-s-gsdp-has-growth-scope-cm-tamang-125041300204_1.html
(retrieved 2026-08-24). This is a ministerial statement, not a published DESME series; the last
figure with a named statistical source remains 7.68% for 2016-17.

**A finding the orchestrator should note: the published hotel register has SHRUNK.**
The 2017 Carrying Capacity Survey counted **1,021** registered hotels. The register ingested into
this repo in 2026 publishes **905** rows (department reports 907). That is a **fall of roughly 11%
over nine years**, during a period when arrivals grew and the department itself was pushing for
"100% registration". Travel agents moved the other way: **1,119 in 2017 → 1,858 in 2026 (+66%)**.
Either hotels have genuinely delisted, or the published register is a filtered subset of the
department's internal list. This asymmetry — agents up two-thirds, hotels down a tenth — is not
explained by any published source found.

### 1.5 Homestay policy

- Governing rules: **Sikkim Registration of Homestay Establishment Rules, 2013** (the 2018 Policy
  §3.1.1 also refers to "Registration of Homestay Establishment Rules (2012)" — the policy is
  internally inconsistent on the year; both references appear in the same document).
- Under the rural tourism push, the Hospitality Division of the Department of Tourism records the
  construction of **736 homestays** (Policy, §3.2 narrative, citing Hospitality Division, GoS).
- **371 homestays were registered with the Department** as of the 2017 survey (Policy §3.3).
  The gap between 736 built and 371 registered is itself in the source document.
- Policy commitments (§5.2): co-financing required for all new department-built homestay
  infrastructure; **leasing out of government-funded homestays is not allowed**; capital and
  interest subsidy plus bank-facilitated finance for local entrepreneurs; capacity building for
  homestay operators, village guides, cooks and porters.
- Registration today runs through a **separate portal**: https://homestay.sikkimtourism.co.in/
  (linked from sikkimtourism.gov.in footer as "Homestay Registration"). **That portal is
  login-gated — it exposes no public homestay register at all** (verified 2026-08-24: the
  unauthenticated page renders only a username/password form). So unlike hotels and travel agents,
  **there is no public directory of registered Sikkim homestays.**
- **No published current count of registered homestays was found** for 2024-2026 from a primary
  government source. Secondary sources cite "around 1,200 registered" without attribution; that
  figure should not be used.

## 2. VERIFIED: does Sikkim legally require a registered travel agent for restricted-area permits?

**Answer: yes for several of the highest-demand destinations, and yes universally for foreign
tourists' Protected Area Permits. But NOT uniformly — for domestic tourists some permits are
issued directly at the Police Check Post. The distinction matters and the product must state it
correctly.**

### 2.1 Primary source

The authoritative text is the Tourism & Civil Aviation Department's own permit guidance published
on sikkimtourism.gov.in. The site is an Angular single-page app; the permit text is not served as
HTML but is embedded in the application bundle `main-PTMWPTUN.js` (877 KB, retrieved 2026-08-24
from https://sikkimtourism.gov.in/main-PTMWPTUN.js). The strings below are quoted verbatim from
that bundle. Human-readable route: https://sikkimtourism.gov.in/Public/TravellerEssentials/rap

### 2.2 Destination-by-destination, quoted

| Destination | Departmental text (verbatim) | Registered agent required? |
|---|---|---|
| **Nathula Pass** | "Permit is issued by the Tourism Department and the vehicle permit by the Police Check Post for domestic tourists, **issued through a registered Travel Agency**." | **YES — including for domestic tourists** |
| **Tsomgo – Baba Mandir** | "Permits are issued by the Police Check Post for domestic tourists. Foreign tourists are allowed till Tsomgo Lake — the permit is issued by the Tourism & Civil Aviation Department and the vehicle permit by the Police Check Post, **applied through a registered Travel Agency**." | **YES for foreigners**; domestic direct at Police Check Post |
| **Gurudongmar** | "Permit is issued by the Police Check Post for domestic tourists. For foreigners, the permit is issued by the Permit Cell, Tourism Department only till Thangu." | **NO explicit agent requirement in the departmental text**; foreigners barred beyond Thangu |
| **Thangu – Chopta Valley** | "…**Foreigners must apply through a Sikkim Tourism registered Travel Agent in groups of two or more**." | **YES for foreigners** |
| **Yumthang / Yumesamdong** | "Permit is issued by the Police Check Post for domestic tourists. For foreigners, the permit is issued by the Tourism Department and the vehicle permit from the Police Check Post." | No explicit agent requirement stated |

### 2.3 The Protected Area Permit rule for foreign nationals

Quoted verbatim from the same departmental bundle, under the RAP/PAP "conditions" block:

> "Through Registered Travel Agents — PAP for a group of two or more foreign tourists can be
> obtained through recognised Travel Agents of Sikkim registered with the Tourism Department,
> **as per the Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Rules, 2008**."

Two further conditions from the same block:
> "On issue of the permit, the foreigner may stay for 30 days unless prior permission for an
> extension is obtained." / "While exiting, the foreigner must present themselves at the FRO
> office to record their exit details."
> "Trekking permits in protected areas are issued free of cost by the Assistant Director, Sikkim
> Tourism Office, Gangtok. Trekkers must also provide a valid physical fitness certificate from a
> competent central / state government medical officer."

**Note the rule citation is to the 2008 Rules, not the 2025 Rules.** As of 2026-08-24 the
department's own public permit page still cites the *Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Rules,
2008* as the legal basis for the registered-agent requirement, even though the *Sikkim
Registration of Tourist Trade Rules, 2025* are in force for the TSD fee (see §4). The public-facing
legal citation has not been updated. That is a documented inconsistency, not an inference.

### 2.4 Product consequence

A public, searchable directory of **licensed** travel agents has **real, legally grounded utility**:
for Nathula (the single most-demanded day trip from Gangtok) a domestic tourist *cannot* obtain the
permit except through a registered travel agency, and every foreign tourist's PAP for Tsomgo,
Thangu–Chopta and the trekking routes must go through a department-registered agent. A tourist who
engages an unregistered operator for these destinations is buying a permit application that cannot
legally be filed.

The counter-consideration, stated honestly: for **Gurudongmar, Yumthang and Yumesamdong** — the
North Sikkim marquee destinations — the departmental text does **not** require a registered agent
for domestic tourists; permits issue at the Police Check Post. The product must not claim a blanket
"you must book through a registered agent" rule. The requirement is destination-specific and
nationality-specific, and the table above is the correct granularity to encode.

## 3. The Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade regime — Act 2024 / Rules 2025

### 3.1 The statutory chain

- **Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Act, 1998** (Act 10 of 1998) — the original statute.
- **Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Rules, 2008** — still the rules cited on the department's
  live public permit page (§2.3).
- **The Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Bill, 2024** — passed by the Sikkim Legislative
  Assembly during the First Session (Part-II) of the Eleventh Assembly, Budget Session 2024-25,
  which commenced 5 August 2024. Bill 23 of 2024.
  Primary text: https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_states/sikkim/2024/Bill23of2024SK.pdf
  (19 pages, scanned; retrieved and page-rendered 2026-08-24). Also mirrored at
  https://sikkimtourism.gov.in/Content/NewImages/files/SRTT%202024.pdf (that URL returns the
  Angular app shell rather than the PDF when fetched directly — use the PRS copy).
  Reporting: https://theprint.in/india/sikkim-assembly-passes-bill-seeking-to-regulate-tourism-industry/2211111/
- **Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Rules, 2025** — the operative subordinate legislation;
  the instrument under which the Rs 50 TSD fee was imposed from March 2025 (§4).
  **The full text of the 2025 Rules was not located at any public URL** during this research.
  Reporting describes their content; the gazette text itself is not published on
  sikkimtourism.gov.in in any form retrievable from outside the SPA.

### 3.2 What registration requires (Act 2024, quoted)

From the rendered scan of Bill 23 of 2024:

- **§6(1) Mandatory registration**: *"Any person intending to run a tourism entity shall, **before
  applying for trade license**, apply for registration with the Department of Tourism and Civil
  Aviation…"* — with a proviso that anyone already operating unregistered at commencement must
  apply **within 30 days**, subject to submitting the trade licence from the concerned authorities.
- **§6(3)**: applications must be disposed of **within 30 days** of receipt.
- **§6(4)**: *"No person shall run a tourism entity unless it is registered… Any person operating
  business or trade without such registration or certificate of commencement as above shall be
  **declared defaulter**."*
- **§7(1) Documents required**: (a) Certificate of Identification or Sikkim Subject Certificate or
  Residential Certificate (wherever applicable); (b) address proof and ID proof; (c) **proof of
  property ownership along with the approved blue print**.
- **§7(3)**: registration certificate issued only **after verification of documents and premises**.
- **§2 definition** of "tourism entity" is deliberately broad: *"hotels, resorts, spa, health and
  wellness facilities, **homestays**, holiday homes, MICE tourism facilities, agencies offering
  business facilitation, event facilitations, **travel agents, tour operators, tour guides**,
  logistic service providers, dealers in tourist merchandize, adventure sports operator, farm based
  tourism service providers…"*

### 3.3 How long a licence is valid, and the renewal obligation

**Critical finding: the Act deliberately does NOT fix a validity period.**

- **§8(1)**: the Prescribed Authority *"shall enlist the name and the particulars of the tourism
  entity operator in the register maintained for the purpose **in physical and electronic form**
  and shall issue a certificate of registration and renewal thereof… **in physical format**"*.
- **§8(2)**: *"**The renewal period and the other requisite formalities shall be prescribed by the
  rules.**"*
- **§9**: hotels/resorts under construction get a **provisional registration certificate for a
  maximum of 3 years or till project completion, whichever is earlier**.

So the licence-validity term lives in the 2025 Rules, whose text is not public (§3.1).
**The statutory validity period of a Sikkim tourist-trade registration could not be verified from
a primary source.** Secondary commercial sources give conflicting answers for Sikkim trade
licences — "valid for one year" and "valid for three years from date of issuance" appear in
different registration-services blogs. **Neither is citable.** Any claim in the product about how
long a hotel or agent licence lasts must therefore be sourced to the register's own per-row
"valid upto" field, not to a stated rule.

### 3.4 Consequences of failure to renew

- **§10(1)(j)**: the Prescribed Authority may cancel registration and **remove the entry from the
  register** where *"the tourism entity operator **fails to renew the registration** as prescribed
  by the rules"* — one of thirteen grounds (a)–(m), alongside child labour (e), premises not
  conforming to standards (f), and being declared a defaulter (m). §10 carries a proviso granting
  a reasonable opportunity of being heard.
- **§4**: the Prescribed Authority may impose penalties and *"in case of repeat of offences,
  initiate **de-registration for a time period or in perpetuity**."*
- **§24**: performing tourism trade not authorised under the Act attracts fine and, on repeat,
  de-registration.
- **§27**: *"Every tourism entity operator shall submit the tariff levied on any tourism activity
  to the Prescribed Authority **on the first day of every Financial Year**."* Charging above the
  prescribed ceiling attracts fine and de-registration.
- **§28(1)(a)**: *"Certificate of Registration is non-transferable"*, with penalties for
  subletting/transferring — the "proxy operations" provision, aimed squarely at the leased-out-hotel
  problem the 2018 Policy identified.

### 3.5 Published enforcement or lapse data

**No published enforcement statistics were found.** No count of de-registrations, defaulter
declarations, fines imposed, inspections conducted, or renewal-compliance rates has been located
in any government publication, press release, assembly answer, or news report reachable in this
research. The department publishes the register itself but not any statistic about how it is
maintained.

This is a substantive negative finding: **the expired-licence proportion computed from the public
register is, as far as this research can establish, the only quantitative statement in existence
about renewal compliance in Sikkim's tourist trade.** That is an argument for the product
publishing it — and an equally strong argument for publishing it with the caveats in §6.

## 4. The ₹50 Tourism Sustainability Development (TSD) fee

### 4.1 Statutory basis

**§11 of the Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Act (Bill 23 of 2024), verbatim:**

> "Tourism Sustainable Development Fund — The State Government under this Act may create a new
> Head of Accounts as Tourism Sustainable Development Fund by levying a nominal fee on tourists and
> other tourism entity operators as prescribed by the rules framed from time to time or by issuing
> notifications under this Act."

Note the Act's own wording is "Tourism **Sustainable** Development Fund"; press coverage and the
department use "Tourism **Sustainability** Development (TSD) Fee/Fund". Both refer to the same
instrument.

### 4.2 Mechanism

Operative from **14 March 2025** under the Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Rules, 2025.

- **Amount: Rs 50 per tourist** — per individual, not per room.
- **Collected by the accommodation unit** — hotels, resorts, homestays, service apartments — at
  the time of booking or check-in. The charge must be explicitly shown in the booking details or
  the unit's registration form.
- **Validity: 30 days** from payment, or until the tourist exits the State, whichever is earlier.
  Re-entry after that requires paying again.
- **Remittance: weekly** transfer by the operator into a designated government account, with
  reporting to the Tourism and Commercial Tax Division.
- **Tracking: the ATITHI Guest Information System (AGIS), Sikkim** is the monitoring platform.
- Tourists receive a "Memento Certificate" on payment.
- Sources: https://sikkimexpress.com/news-details/sikkim-introduces-rs50-entry-fee-for-tourists-to-fund-sustainable-tourism-development
  (retrieved 2026-08-24); https://www.uniindia.com/news/east/tourism-sikkim-entry-fee/3414026.html;
  https://travelbizmonitor.com/top-stories/sikkim-introduces-inr-50-tourist-entry-fee/

### 4.3 Exemptions

- Children **below five years of age**.
- **Government employees / persons visiting on official government duty**.
- **Local residents**, on production of valid identity or official documents.
- Source: Sikkim Express, as above (retrieved 2026-08-24).

### 4.4 Has any collection total been published?

**No published collection total was found.** No figure for TSD Fund receipts — cumulative, annual,
monthly, or by district — has been located in any budget document, press release, assembly answer
or news report. Reporting on the scheme covers only its introduction and the debate over it
(e.g. https://www.business-standard.com/politics/sikkim-s-rs-50-tourism-toll-sparks-debate-over-cost-sustainability-125032300793_1.html,
23 March 2025).

**Do not compute an implied total.** It is arithmetically tempting to multiply 17,12,360 arrivals
by Rs 50 and quote ~Rs 8.56 crore. That number would be wrong and must not appear in the product:
the fee began mid-March 2025 (so ~9.5 months of 2025 arrivals, not 12), exempts under-fives,
official visitors and residents, is capped at one payment per 30-day stay, and depends entirely on
collection compliance by accommodation units — for which no compliance rate is published. Any such
figure is a fabrication with a plausible shape. **The honest statement is: the TSD Fund's receipts
have never been published.**
