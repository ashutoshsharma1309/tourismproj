# Sikkim Darshan → the tourism trade

**SIH 2026 · Problem Statement 26202 · Student Innovation · AICTE, MIC**
*"A solution/idea that can boost the current situation of the tourism industries
including hotels, travel and others."*

Every figure below was measured against this repository on 2026-08-24 and can be
re-measured with the commands in [§9](#9--how-to-check-every-number-here).

---

## 1 · How we read the statement

Three words carry the whole thing.

**"Industries."** Plural, and *industries* — not *tourists*. The statement names
the supply side: the businesses that constitute tourism. It then names two of
them explicitly, hotels and travel, and leaves "others" deliberately open.

**"Boost."** A change verb. Not *showcase*, not *digitize*. Something has to be
measurably better afterwards.

**"The current situation."** The load-bearing phrase, and the one most entries
will fail. You cannot claim to have improved a situation you cannot first
*state*. So the question that organises this project is not "what could we
build for tourists?" but:

> **What is the actual, present, measurable condition of Sikkim's tourism
> trade — and who is in a position to say?**

## 2 · The finding

The Government of Sikkim already registers, vets and publishes its entire
tourism industry. The Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade Act defines a
"tourism entity" as — quoting §2 —

> *"hotels, resorts, spa, health and wellness facilities, **homestays**,
> holiday homes, MICE tourism facilities, agencies offering business
> facilitation, event facilitations, **travel agents, tour operators, tour
> guides**, logistic service providers, dealers in tourist merchandize,
> adventure sports operator, farm based tourism service providers…"*

That is the problem statement's *"hotels, travel and others"*, enumerated in
statute. The registers exist. **They are also, in practice, unreadable.**

The department publishes them as paginated tables inside an Angular bundle with
no JSON API — 75 pages for the travel agents alone — with no search, no
district filter, and no way to query them. `curl` returns an app shell. So we
rendered the pages and read the DOM, which is the only way to get the data out.

What came out:

| | Hotels | Travel agencies |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Entries on the register | **905** (dept. reports 907) | **1,858** (matches dept. total) |
| Carrying a grade from the state | 22 | 70 |
| Carrying **no** grade | **883** | **1,788** |
| Past their printed validity date | **817 of 897** (91.1%) | **1,747 of 1,850** (94.4%) |
| Reachable — phone on the register | 795 | 1,706 |

**2,763 licensed businesses. 2,564 entries past their printed validity date.
2,671 carrying no grade at all.** Not one of them searchable.

### The dispersal gap

Cross-referencing those registers against this project's 53 sourced heritage
sites — two government datasets nobody currently publishes together:

| District | Heritage | Hotels | Agencies | Index |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Gangtok | 26.4% | **74.6%** | **83.3%** | 2.82 |
| Mangan | **24.5%** | **2.2%** | **0.9%** | **0.09** |
| Gyalshing | 18.9% | 11.3% | 6.8% | 0.60 |
| Namchi | 17.0% | 5.6% | 5.0% | 0.33 |
| Soreng | 7.5% | 1.1% | 1.8% | 0.15 |
| Pakyong | 5.7% | 5.2% | 2.2% | 0.92 |

**Sikkim's heritage is distributed. Its tourism industry is not.** Mangan holds
very nearly as much catalogued heritage as Gangtok and about a thirty-fourth of
its registered hotel supply.

## 3 · Why this is worth publishing

Because nobody else has. Our research established a hard negative:

> **No count of renewals, de-registrations, inspections, fines or
> compliance rates appears in any Government of Sikkim publication, press
> release, assembly answer or news report we could locate.** The department
> publishes the register; it publishes no statistic about how the register is
> maintained.

Which makes the 91% and 94% figures above, as far as we can establish, **the
only quantitative statement about renewal compliance in Sikkim's tourist trade
that exists in public.**

That is a strong claim, so it ships with strong constraints — see §5.

## 4 · What we built

Three surfaces on one thesis, all on the provenance machinery this project
already ran on. Nothing existing was removed.

1. **`/industry` — both registers, searchable.** 2,763 establishments,
   filterable by type, district and registration status, searchable by name or
   registration number. The first time this data has been queryable outside a
   government SPA.
2. **The capacity table.** Heritage share against registered-supply share, by
   district — server-rendered, so it is in the HTML whether or not JavaScript
   runs.
3. **Licence currency as a first-class field**, phrased as a statement about a
   register entry and never about a business.

Plus the permit link that makes the agency register load-bearing rather than
decorative: the department routes **Nathula, Tsomgo, Singalila, Green Lake and
Maenam** permits *through a registered travel agency*. For Nathula that binds
domestic visitors too. A tourist engaging an unregistered operator for those
destinations is buying a permit application that cannot legally be filed — and
until now had no searchable way to check.

That list is **derived from the permit records, not hardcoded**, so if the
department changes a permit route the page changes with it.

## 5 · The discipline, and why it is the differentiator

This project's standing rule is *every factual claim points at a source or it
does not ship*. Applied to businesses, that rule got stricter, because the cost
of an error changed. A wrong founding year on a monastery is an error. A wrong
licence state against a named hotel with a published telephone number is closer
to a defamation.

So three limits are enforced **mechanically**, by `npm run qa:industry`
(25/25 passing), not by good intentions:

- **A statement about a register is never rendered as a statement about a
  business.** The QA harness scans the rendering layer for the words that would
  cross that line — *unlicensed*, *illegal*, *closed down*, *avoid this* — and
  fails the build if one reaches a visitor-facing string. It caught our own copy
  once, and we rewrote the sentence rather than weakening the check.
- **An absent grade never renders as a bad grade.** 2,671 of 2,763 entries carry
  no grade; the register states none, and the UI shows none.
- **Licence currency is assessed against the date the register was read, not
  against today** — because "today" silently assumes the register has not
  changed since, and would make a prerendered page's content depend on when the
  build ran. The QA harness fails if `new Date()` appears in that path.

And the caveats travel with the numbers in the product, not in a footnote:
expired ≠ closed; the register may simply not be rewritten on renewal; the
heritage denominator is *our* 53 catalogued sites, not an inventory of Sikkim;
and Mangan's thin supply may record the October 2023 Teesta flood — which
destroyed thirteen bridges and much of the connectivity infrastructure along the
river — rather than an opening.

**We could not verify how long a Sikkim registration lasts.** §8(2) of the Act
defers it to the 2025 Rules, whose text is not published anywhere public. So the
product makes no claim about it and uses only the per-row date the register
prints. That gap is stated rather than filled.

## 6 · What we refused to build

Named explicitly, because the refusals are the argument.

| Not built | Why |
| --- | --- |
| An AI chatbot | The product already answers hallucination better than a chatbot does — by *retrieving* instead of generating. The trip guide has no model in it and cannot invent a founder, a date or a price. Bolting an LLM on would destroy the differentiator, not add one. |
| Booking / payments | No licensed inventory or tariff feed exists. Building it means inventing prices. This project shipped invented tariffs once and deleted them; doing it again to win a hackathon forfeits the only thing that makes the data credible. |
| Ratings and reviews | Same failure, same history. The state grades 92 of 2,763 businesses; we are not going to rate the other 2,671. |
| 360° / AR tours | A sweep of 35 sites established that no openly licensed 360° capture of any Sikkim monastery exists — Commons has no such category for this state. The constraint is the world's, not the code's. |
| "Are you a first-time visitor?" onboarding | Intent is inferable from search and filters. A quiz is not a product. |

## 7 · Why an ordinary tourism website cannot do this

- **MakeMyTrip / Booking.com** list bookable inventory. 883 of Sikkim's 905
  registered hotels have no grade and most have no online presence; they are
  commercially invisible and will stay that way.
- **Google Maps** knows where a hotel is. It does not know whether its
  registration is current, and it holds no relationship to a heritage inventory.
- **The official portal** *has* the data and cannot surface it — no search, no
  filter, no API, 75 pages of pagination.
- **None of them** can compute a heritage-against-supply figure, because none of
  them holds both halves. That join is only available to something that is
  already a sourced heritage archive *and* has read the licensing registers.

Which is the honest answer to *why does this need to exist*: not because it is a
prettier tourism site, but because it is the only artefact positioned to make
that particular join, and the join is the finding.

## 8 · Impact, and what a department would actually get

Measurable without inventing a baseline, because every figure is recomputed from
the registers on each ingest:

- **2,763 licensed businesses made findable** — 1,858 of them (the entire travel
  agent register) published for the first time in usable form.
- **A renewal-compliance figure** where none is currently published, per district
  and per register.
- **A dispersal instrument**: heritage against registered supply, per district,
  recomputed whenever either side changes.
- **A permit-to-operator path** for the five destinations the state routes
  through registered agencies.

For the department, the refresh path is two commands
(`npm run ingest:tourism` → `npm run registers:promote`) and every promotion is
reproducible and diffable. For an operator, being on the register is now worth
something publicly. For a visitor, the question *"who can legally take me to
Nathula?"* has an answer for the first time.

## 10 · Demo path — four minutes

1. **The statement, and the register.** Open the department's travel-agent page.
   Show the pagination. 75 pages, no search. *"1,858 licensed businesses the
   state has already vetted. Try finding one."*
2. **`/industry`.** Same data, one search box. Type "Nathula" context — filter to
   Mangan, filter to current registrations. *This is the boost: the state did the
   vetting, we did the retrieval.*
3. **The capacity table.** Heritage against supply. Mangan: 24.5% / 2.2%. Let it
   sit. Then read the caveat aloud — including the flood — because the caveat is
   the credibility.
4. **The licence panel.** 91% and 94%. Then: *"and no government publication
   anywhere states this, which is why we are careful about what it means."*
5. **Close on the QA run.** `npm run qa:industry` — 25/25, including a check that
   fails the build if any string in the product calls a business unlicensed.
   *We enforce our own honesty in CI.*

## 11 · Remaining risks

- **The 2025 Rules are unpublished**, so licence-term semantics remain
  unverifiable. Stated in-product rather than papered over.
- **The registers are a snapshot** read on 2026-08-18. Everything is assessed
  against that date, and the date is on the page.
- **The heritage denominator is 53 catalogued sites**, not all of Sikkim. The
  index is a ratio of shares within what this archive holds, and says so.
- **Expired-entry share is not a compliance rate.** It is the share of published
  entries past a printed date. We have been unable to find anyone who publishes
  the real compliance rate, including the department.
- **The external research is 4 of 8 planned sections** — the competitive matrix
  and the small-operator interviews were not completed. What is cited is sourced;
  what is missing is named here rather than improvised.
