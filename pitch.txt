───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PART 1 · THE PROBLEM, STATED PLAINLY
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Sikkim has around 200 monasteries. They hold the state's art, its festival
calendar, its written record and much of its history. They are also fragile:
Phodong was levelled by earthquake and rebuilt, Sanga Choeling has burned more
than once, Phensang burned in 1947.

Almost none of this exists in a form that survives, or that a visitor can use.

Concretely, we went and checked:

  • The Government of Sikkim tourism portal publishes NO visiting hours for any
    monastery. Not one. That negative finding is itself a cited source in our
    registry.
  • No 360° photograph of any Sikkim monastery exists in any openly licensed
    collection. Wikimedia Commons has a "360° panoramas" category for
    neighbouring states and none for Sikkim. We swept 35 sites to be sure.
  • Only 8 of our 15 catalogued monasteries have a coordinate published by an
    authoritative source.
  • Travel aggregators fill the vacuum with numbers that contradict each other.
    Reported opening times for Rumtek alone range from 6am to 10am, closing from
    5pm to 6pm, and none of them cites the monastery or the department.
  • Two of Sikkim's four official languages — Bhutia and Lepcha — have no
    speech-synthesis voice in any engine on earth. The languages of the people
    whose heritage this is cannot currently be spoken by a machine at all.

So the problem is not "Sikkim needs a tourism website." The problem is that the
information a visitor needs, and the record a culture needs, largely does not
exist in retrievable form — and the vacuum is being filled with guesses.

PART 2 · THE MVP — WHAT IS ACTUALLY BUILT AND RUNNING
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

One sentence: Sikkim Darshan is a digital cultural archive of Sikkim, built on a
rule the data model enforces —

    Every claim on the site is traceable to a named source,
    or the site says it does not know.

Not "we try to be accurate." A structural guarantee. Every record carries
sourceId, sourceUrl, verifiedAt and a confidence grade. A field with no source
renders as "Not published" / "Documentation currently unavailable", never as a
plausible-looking value.

The MVP is six connected collections plus a public audit surface. All of it is
live, prerendered and works with no API key and no network.

  190 prerendered routes across 16 route templates.

  15 monasteries — district, lineage, founding year, history, architecture,
     significance, provenance badge on every record
  14 of 15 cite a specific reference article (per-record URL, not a blanket
     citation). The 15th, Kewzing, ships marked "Unverified" rather than dressed

  26 documented events across 6 eras, from the crowning at Yuksom in 1642 to

  16 of 26 carry a legitimately sourced photograph. The other 10 say
     "Documentation currently unavailable", which is true and better than a

PART 3 · WHY WE ARE BETTER
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every other entry in this problem statement will show you a monastery website.
Six things separate ours.

3.1 THE HONESTY IS IN THE TYPE SYSTEM, NOT THE PITCH
    Anyone can say "our data is accurate." Our records cannot hold a fact
    without a source URL, a verification date and a confidence grade, and a QA
    script fails the build if a provenance rule is broken. The guarantee is
    mechanical. You can test it in front of us.

3.2 NO AI WRITES A SINGLE PUBLISHED SENTENCE
    Most heritage demos this year are an LLM wrapper. Ours is the opposite: the
    agents research, extract, score and measure, and every published clause is
    assembled from a verified field. A hallucinated founding year is not a bug
    we hope to avoid — it is structurally unreachable. That is the difference
    between a demo and something a Tourism Department can put its name on.

3.3 WE MEASURED WHAT OTHERS SCORE
    Asked to rate our narration on naturalness, we did not print "8.7/10." We
    said we cannot hear it, then built an eleven-candidate, four-language voice
    benchmark and measured what is measurable — word error rate, loudness,
    pace, clipping. That work paid for itself twice:
      · The first instrument was lying. Whisper-small put Hindi at 61–72% WER;
        Whisper-medium halved it, and the RANKING INVERTED — the incumbent
        macOS Lekha voice won at 31.5% WER over all three Piper candidates.
        Trusting pass one would have shipped a LESS intelligible voice and
        called it an upgrade.
      · Nepali and Bengali stayed above 100% WER on both models — the
        recogniser invents more words than the script contains. So we published
        "unmeasurable here" instead of a ranking that would have been noise
        dressed as data.
    One real defect was found and fixed: Bengali was 4.5 dB louder than
    English, so changing language jumped in your ears. RMS-matched to a 0.2 dB
    spread, gain only — no compression, EQ or reverb, because heavy processing
    is what makes synthetic speech worse.

3.4 WE DELETED OUR MOST IMPRESSIVE FEATURES
    An earlier build had 100 guest reviews, a revenue dashboard, occupancy
    charts, a fabricated TSD remittance ledger, invented festival dates and
    phone numbers, five 360° tours, and a headline of 2.1 million tourists. All
    generated. We audited our own project and deleted every bit of it rather
    than relabelling it.
    Two of those numbers were not filler, they were WRONG ABOUT PUBLIC FACT: we
    showed Sikkim's levy as a percentage of the room bill (it is a flat ₹50 per
    person at check-in), and our arrivals figure was ~25% too high. Any judge
    from Sikkim would catch both in four seconds.
    The site got smaller and true.

3.5 WE PUBLISH OUR OWN FAILURES ON A PUBLIC PAGE
    /preservation reports 0 of 15 for 360° captures and 1 of 15 for visiting
    hours, next to 15 of 15 for audio. No competitor will show you their
    coverage gaps, because their gaps are filled with invention. Ours is the
    page we ask to be judged on.

3.6 IT IS AN ARCHIVE, NOT A BROCHURE — AND THE COMMUNITY CAN ADD TO IT
    Timeline, object archive, 70 stories, 19 categories, 7 communities, a
    curator-gated contribution route, and cross-links between all of it. A
    brochure is finished when the hackathon ends. An archive with an intake
    queue is a thing the state can keep filling for twenty years.

PART 4 · WHY USERS ACTUALLY NEED THIS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE VISITOR STANDING IN GANGTOK, DECIDING WHERE TO GO
  Today they get aggregator pages disagreeing about opening hours, no idea which
  monastery is a working community versus a museum piece, and no way to know
  which of the two hundred is worth their one free day. We give them 15 sites
  with sourced history, a courtyard video, an audio guide in their own language
  and a plain statement of what is not known. 17,12,360 people arrived in Sikkim
  in 2025. Almost none of them had this.

THE VISITOR WHO DOES NOT READ ENGLISH COMFORTABLY
  16,35,650 of those 2025 arrivals were domestic. Nepali is Sikkim's most spoken
  language; the West Bengal corridor is how most of them travel in. Sixty
  narrated guides in English, Hindi, Nepali and Bengali, each with a transcript,
  is a real accessibility unlock — not a language dropdown that machine-
  translates an English page.

THE PERSON WHO CANNOT MAKE THE CLIMB
  Dubdi is a 35-minute steep trek above Yuksom. Sikkim's monasteries ask a lot
  of a body, and they ask it at altitude. The elderly, the disabled and the
  diaspora who will never fly back get the history, the murals, the narration
  and the sacred landscape from a screen. The mountains ask a lot. The archive
  asks a screen.

THE TOURISM DEPARTMENT AND THE STATE
  A department cannot publish a claim it cannot defend. Everything here arrives
  with its source attached, its confidence graded and its gaps listed, so it can
  be adopted, corrected or extended without a fact-check project first. We would
  rather hand them 15 monasteries they can stake their name on than 200 they
  would have to check.

THE MONASTERIES AND THE COMMUNITIES THEMSELVES
  Phodong was levelled by an earthquake and rebuilt. Sanga Choeling has burned
  more than once. Phensang burned in 1947. Documentation is the cheapest
  insurance against the next time — and the contribution route means a family
  with a photograph, or a monastery with its own festival calendar, has somewhere
  to put it where it will be credited and kept.

RESEARCHERS, TEACHERS AND WIKIPEDIA ITSELF
  A 26-event timeline with 50 citations that records where sources disagree, and
  67 licensed objects with creator and licence intact, is a citable starting
  point. Sikkim's monastic record currently has no single one.

PART 5 · THE ZEROS, AND WHY THEY ARE THE PITCH
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Sites with a true 360° sphere ............ 0 of 15
Sites with OFFICIAL visiting hours ....... 0 of 15  (1 has "reported" hours)
Invented guest reviews ................... 0
Invented prices, ratings, occupancy ...... 0
LLM-written published sentences .......... 0

None of those is a feature we ran out of time on. Each one is a RESULT:

  · No openly licensed 360° sphere of any Sikkim monastery exists — Commons has
    the category for neighbouring states and not for Sikkim. Filtering by aspect
    ratio alone produced a close-up of a lily and a snow leopard, which is why
    every candidate was opened and looked at.
  · Street View availability cannot be established without a Maps Platform key,
    and the only sanctioned check is the metadata endpoint. With no key, the
    build records "not checked" rather than guessing.
  · The tourism department publishes no hours for any monastery. We checked, and
    filed the negative as a source.

So the 360 viewer is built, tested, and ships empty — and tells the visitor
exactly why. The one real capture we found ships as the 180° photograph it is.

Here is the argument. A tourism site with fifty fake panoramas is worth LESS
than one with zero, because the moment a visitor catches a single invention,
every other claim becomes suspect. For a cultural heritage archive, credibility
is not a feature. It is the entire product.

PART 7 · ANTICIPATED JUDGE QUESTIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Q: Only 15 monasteries out of 200?
A: 15 we can defend. Our discovery agent found 35 articles and 22 more
   candidates sit in the review queue with their evidence attached. Scaling is a
   review-capacity problem now, not an engineering one — and a catalogue of 200
   unverified records is a liability for a government, not an asset.

Q: Why is there no 360° tour? That is in the problem statement.
A: Because no licensed 360° capture of any Sikkim monastery exists. We swept 35
   sites and filed the negative. The viewer is built and ships empty with the
   reason printed. The alternative — which our own earlier build did — was to
   show a European panorama as a Sikkim prayer hall.

Q: Where is Wikipedia's authority? It is not a primary source.
A: Agreed, and that is why encyclopedia-sourced confidence is capped at
   "medium", agreement between two articles in the same work does not upgrade a
   record, and the timeline reserves "verified" for government and institutional
   sources. The cap is enforced by a QA script, not by discipline.

Q: The audio is machine-generated. Is that acceptable for sacred content?
A: It is disclosed in the player on every guide, the transcript is published in
   the same language, no model wrote the words, and languages we cannot do
   properly are listed as blocked rather than faked. Human narration is the
   named next step for Bhutia and Lepcha.

Q: How do you know your narration is any good?
A: We measured it and published both the measurements and their limits — see
   VOICE-QUALITY-REPORT.md and voice-benchmark/listen.html. We also caught our
   own measuring instrument lying, which reversed a voice decision.

Q: What is your AI/ML contribution if no model writes anything?
A: Eight agents that discover, cross-check, licence-verify, score confidence,
   render speech, gate language purity, measure the resulting waveform, and
   route everything uncertain to a human. The intelligence is in verification,
   not generation. Verification is the harder half and the half this domain
   needs.

Q: Is this deployable?
A: It builds clean to 190 prerendered routes and runs with zero environment
   variables. Supabase is an optional overlay with RLS. It works offline of any
   service.

Q: Stays with no prices and no ratings — is that not a worse product?
A: It is a smaller true product. No licensed feed supplies them here. The
   earlier build invented tariffs and reviews, and inventing a hotel price is
   how you lose the right to be believed about a monastery.
