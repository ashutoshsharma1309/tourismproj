PART 1 · UNDERSTAND THE PROJECT
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1.1 THE PROBLEM, STATED PLAINLY

Sikkim has around 200 monasteries. They hold the state's art, its festival
calendar, its written record and much of its history. They are also fragile:
Phodong was levelled by earthquake and rebuilt, Sanga Choeling has burned more
than once, Phensang burned in 1947.

Almost none of this is documented in a form that survives.

Concretely, we found:
  • The Government of Sikkim tourism portal publishes NO visiting hours for
    any monastery. We checked.
  • No 360° photograph of any Sikkim monastery exists in any openly licensed
    collection. We swept 35 sites to establish this.
  • Only 8 of our 15 catalogued monasteries have a coordinate published by an
    authoritative source.
  • Travel aggregators fill these gaps with numbers that contradict each
    other. Reported opening times for Rumtek alone range from 6am to 10am.

So the problem is not "Sikkim needs a tourism website." The problem is that
the information a visitor needs, and the record a culture needs, largely does
not exist in retrievable form — and the vacuum is being filled with guesses.


1.2 WHAT THIS PROJECT IS

A digital cultural archive for Sikkim's monasteries, with a strict rule:

    Every claim on the site is traceable to a named source,
    or the site says it does not know.

Not "we try to be accurate." A structural guarantee. The data model itself
cannot hold an unsourced fact — every record carries sourceId, sourceUrl,
verifiedAt and a confidence grade. A field with no source renders as "Not
published" or "Not yet verified", never as a plausible-looking value.

1.4 THE AGENT PIPELINE

Four autonomous agents, runnable as npm scripts:

  npm run agent:discover  Heritage Discovery Agent
      Enumerates the Wikipedia monastery category rather than working from a
      guessed list. Found 35 monastery articles against our 15. Pulls prose,
      coordinate and licence-checked image per record. Scores on evidence:
      VERIFIED / PARTIALLY_VERIFIED / NEEDS_REVIEW.

  npm run agent:360      360 Heritage Finder
      Searches licensed panorama sources, filters to equirectangular signature
      (≥3000px, 2:1 aspect), distance-scores candidates against the site
      coordinate. Street View is checked only via the official metadata
      endpoint — scraping the Maps site would breach its terms.

  npm run agent:audio    Heritage Audio Agent
      Composes each language natively from verified fields, runs a language-
      purity gate BEFORE synthesis, renders, then measures the actual signal
      (peak, RMS, silence ratio, clipping) and rejects failures.

  npm run agent:report   Data Quality Agent
      Aggregates coverage and gaps into reports/monastery-data-quality.json

Critically: no agent can publish. Output lands in a review queue.

PART 2 · THE THINGS WE DELETED
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This is the most important section, and the one judges will remember.

An earlier build of this project was more impressive-looking. It had:
  • ~100 guest reviews with names and star ratings
  • hotel tariffs, occupancy figures, a revenue dashboard
  • a TSD remittance ledger with 220 bookings
  • 360° virtual tours on five monasteries
  • "2,147,892 tourists in 2025"

All of it was fabricated. We audited our own work and removed every piece.

Two of those numbers were worse than filler — they misstated real public fact:

  1. THE TSD LEVY
     We showed it as 2–4% of room tariff.
     It is actually a FLAT ₹50 PER PERSON, collected by hotels at check-in,
     valid one month, under-5s exempt — Sikkim Registration of Tourist Trade
     Rules, 2025. The fund is real. Our maths was invented.
     A judge from Sikkim would have caught this instantly.

  2. TOURIST ARRIVALS
     We showed 21,47,892 for 2025.
     The reported figure is 17,12,360 (16,35,650 domestic + 61,710 foreign).
     We were ~25% high.

  3. THE 360° TOURS
     Five monasteries showed "360° tours" that were actually the same two
     panoramas — of Paulinzella Abbey in GERMANY and a temple in
     SWITZERLAND — reused across Sikkim monasteries.

The site went from 44 routes to 22. That is the honest size of the verified
dataset. We would rather demo a smaller true thing.

PART 3 · THE ZEROS, AND WHY THEY ARE THE PITCH
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  360° experiences published ......... 0 of 15
  Monasteries with official hours .... 0 of 15
  Guest reviews ...................... 0
  Assamese / Dzongkha audio .......... 0

These are not unfinished work. Each is an evidenced negative result:

  360°: 35 sites swept. No licensed panorama of any Sikkim monastery exists.
        Street View cannot even be CHECKED without a Google Maps Platform key,
        and the metadata endpoint is the only sanctioned way to check.
        → The viewer is built. It ships empty, and says so.

  HOURS: The official tourism portal publishes none — we fetched it to
        confirm. Aggregators contradict each other. Rumtek shows "reported
        hours, confirm locally"; the other 14 say "not published".

  ASSAMESE/DZONGKHA: No speech voice exists on any engine available to us.
        Dzongkha has a second blocker — we have no Dzongkha speaker to review
        a translation about Buddhist practice.

The preservation dashboard PUBLISHES these gaps. It separates "capability
built" from "content captured", so nothing reads as finished because a
component exists.

A tourism site with 50 fake panoramas is worth less than one with zero and an
honest empty state — because the moment a visitor catches one invention, every
other claim on the site becomes suspect. For a CULTURAL HERITAGE archive, that
credibility is the entire product.

PART 5 · ANTICIPATED JUDGE QUESTIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Q: "Why does your 360° section show nothing? Isn't that the main feature?"
A: Because no 360° photograph of a Sikkim monastery exists under an open
   licence — we swept 35 sites to prove it. The viewer is built and tested.
   What's missing is a camera in Sikkim, not code. Every competitor showing
   360° tours of Sikkim monasteries is either licensed from someone, or
   showing you somewhere else.

Q: "Only 15 monasteries? Sikkim has 200."
A: 15 verified, with per-record citations. 22 more sit in the review queue
   with their evidence attached, awaiting human approval. We can publish 200
   tomorrow — we won't publish 200 unverified.

Q: "Isn't the AI narration just a gimmick?"
A: The scripts are assembled from verified database fields, not generated
   prose, so the narration structurally cannot hallucinate a founder or a
   date. It's disclosed as machine-generated, has a full transcript, and runs
   in four languages including Nepali — Sikkim's most spoken language.

Q: "What's genuinely novel here?"
A: The provenance architecture. Most heritage sites are a CMS with content in
   it. Ours cannot store an unsourced claim, publishes its own coverage gaps,
   and separates what an agent found from what a human approved.

Q: "How do we know YOUR data is right?"
A: Click any source link. Every fact on the site has one, and the source
   registry is a page on the site — /preservation lists all six sources and
   exactly what may be cited from each.
